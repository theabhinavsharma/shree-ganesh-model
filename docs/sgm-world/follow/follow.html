The journey films, on the real Earth

Each film follows one bot's whole run in about 30 seconds, from the place its data comes from to your laptop in Bengaluru. "Start the journey" on the home page now plays these. They are recordings of the page running on the laptop, frame by frame.

Not live. shreeganeshmodel.vercel.app is unchanged until you say go.

What happens, beat by beat

    How the camera works

    • The bots are the size of a person, about 1.8 m.
    • Street level: for the first and last stretch of each road, the camera runs just behind the bot, at running pace, through Google's 3D map of the real street.
    • The long middle: the camera climbs like a drone and the run speeds up into a time-lapse. Further from the ground, the bot is shown larger than life so it never shrinks to a dot.
    • A bright streak stays on every road already covered. The submarine's sea leg is cyan, and the tanker leaves a white wake. The dashed white line is the route still ahead.

    What's real and what's simplified

    • Real: the buildings and streets (Google's 3D map), and the roads (OpenStreetMap). That's 1,016 km Frankfurt to Marseille, 1,532 km St. Louis to the New Jersey shore, and 996 km Mumbai to Bengaluru.
    • Simplified: the sea routes follow the undersea-cable paths, drawn as a submarine and a tanker. The real data takes a fraction of a second through the cable.
    • Every stop is a public place: the ECB, the St. Louis Fed, Prado beach, Long Branch beach, Versova beach and Vidhana Soudha. No home address is shown.
    • Each film ends with what the data is used for. Exchange rates are context only; Fed's prices feed Friday's rising and fading themes.

    On the live site

    • Start the journey: the crew lines up. Press Go! and Euro's film plays. At the end: watch Fed's, watch again, or explore.
    • Map loading: while the crew is on screen, the page loads the street-level spots, so the film starts sharp.
    • Sky: films play in daylight (Fed's at dawn in St. Louis and sunset in India). Exploring the globe keeps the real time of day.
    • Bot cards: each bot with a film has a "Watch its journey" button. Links like /#follow-fed open a film directly.

    Limits

    • Google's 3D map is built from aerial photos. Trees and parked cars right beside the camera look soft, so the camera stays a few metres up.
    • On a slow connection the first street shot can be soft for a moment while tiles finish loading.
    • Films exist for Euro and Fed so far. The other bots appear on the globe and the crew page.
    • Not yet checked on a real phone.
