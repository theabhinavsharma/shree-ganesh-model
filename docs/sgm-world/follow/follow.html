Euro's journey, on the real Earth

The follow-cam preview: one bot's whole run in about 30 seconds. It starts at the European Central Bank in Frankfurt and ends in your laptop in Bengaluru. This is a recording of the page running on the laptop, frame by frame.

How the camera works

  • The bot is the size of a person, about 1.8 m.
  • Street level: for the first and last stretch of each road, the camera runs just behind the bot, at running pace, through Google's 3D map of the real street.
  • The long middle: the camera climbs like a drone and the run speeds up into a time-lapse. Further from the ground, the bot is shown larger than life so it never shrinks to a dot.
  • A bright streak stays on every road the crate has covered. The sea leg is cyan. The dashed white line is the route still ahead.

What's real and what's simplified

  • Real: the buildings and streets (Google's 3D map), and the road routes (OpenStreetMap, 1,016 km and 996 km of actual road).
  • Simplified: the sea route follows the undersea-cable path, drawn as a submarine. The real data takes a fraction of a second through the cable.
  • The start (ECB), the beaches (Prado in Marseille, Versova in Mumbai) and Vidhana Soudha in Bengaluru are public places. No home address is shown.
  • Exchange rates are kept as market context. They don't pick stocks; the film says so at the end.

Limits

  • Google's 3D map is built from aerial photos. At street level, trees and parked cars right next to the camera look soft, so the camera stays a few metres up.
  • On a slow connection the map can look blurry for a moment until its tiles load. The page loads the four street-level spots before the film starts.
